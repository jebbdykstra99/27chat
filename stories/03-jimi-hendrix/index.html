Jimi Hendrix's Work: From the R&B Road to Electric Lady, and the Legends

By Jebb Dykstra, Juno, Vesta and Iris Human + AI

27chat.com · Story 03 · The work didn't stop.

Jimi Hendrix released three studio albums with the Jimi Hendrix Experience and a live album with Band of Gypsys in about four years, then left a double album unfinished. This story follows that work, from the R&B road to Electric Lady Studios, and then sets the legends next to the record.

What did Jimi Hendrix do before he was famous?

Before he was famous, Hendrix was a working sideman on the R&B circuit, often called the chitlin' circuit. The official Hendrix biography says that by the end of 1965 he had played with Ike and Tina Turner, Sam Cooke, the Isley Brothers and Little Richard. NPR's Ed Ward traced him joining the Isleys' road band in 1964, touring in a package show with Sam Cooke and Jackie Wilson, and recording with Little Richard. In New York he led his own group, Jimmy James and the Blue Flames.

How did Chas Chandler bring Jimi Hendrix to London?

Chas Chandler, the Animals' bassist, saw him at Cafe Wha? in Greenwich Village in summer 1966 and brought him to London in September. The BBC reported that Chandler won him over by promising to introduce him to Eric Clapton. Chandler changed "Jimmy" to "Jimi" and teamed him with drummer Mitch Mitchell and bassist Noel Redding. The Jimi Hendrix Experience's first single, "Hey Joe," reached No. 6 in the UK in early 1967.

Why is Are You Experienced in the National Recording Registry?

The Library of Congress added Are You Experienced (1967) to the National Recording Registry in 2005. In the Library's essay, Bill Hart writes that some reviewers at the time already heard Hendrix "changing the very language of popular music." Hart describes a style that ran from Curtis Mayfield-style chords to an "incendiary, jazzy and complex" lead, kept inside three-minute songs. He also points out that the UK edition included the 12-bar blues "Red House," while the US edition swapped in the hits.

What happened at Monterey and Woodstock?

At Monterey, on June 18, 1967, the Experience made its American debut, and it made Hendrix a star at home. The official site calls the Monterey debut "triumphant," and the Library of Congress essay says it led to his US signing. At Woodstock, on Monday morning, August 18, 1969, he led a six-piece band called Gypsy Sun and Rainbows, and his set included the "Star Spangled Banner." The official site describes that performance as a three-and-a-half-minute improvisation.

What were Electric Ladyland, Electric Lady Studios and Band of Gypsys?

Electric Ladyland was a double album released in October 1968. Electric Lady Studios was the recording studio he built in Greenwich Village, which officially opened on August 26, 1970. In between, he formed Band of Gypsys with Billy Cox on bass and Buddy Miles on drums. Their four shows at the Fillmore East on December 31, 1969 and January 1, 1970 became the album Band of Gypsys, released in March 1970. Cox, quoted on the official site, said songs like "Machine Gun" "were created just for the Band Of Gypsys."

What was First Rays of the New Rising Sun?

First Rays of the New Rising Sun was the double album Hendrix was making at Electric Lady in 1970 and never finished. According to the official site's history of the album, even Mitchell and Cox could not predict which songs he would have chosen. The 1997 release, prepared by his family and engineer Eddie Kramer, gathers 17 songs from the master tapes, some of which "lacked only his final touches."

How did Jimi Hendrix die?

Hendrix died in London on September 18, 1970, aged 27. The inquest recorded an open verdict after hearing that he died from inhaling vomit after barbiturate intoxication.

Jimi Hendrix legends and disputed stories

These stories are labeled as legend or dispute, and each is set beside what is documented.

Disputed: the timeline of his last morning

The dispute: Monika Dannemann, from whose west London home he was taken by ambulance, told the 1970 inquest she found him sleeping, went out for cigarettes, came back and saw something was wrong. In December 1993, the Independent reported "apparent discrepancies over the time of death," and that she was answering allegations that he "died hours before I said he did." Kathy Etchingham, a former girlfriend, criticized Dannemann's account for its "inconsistencies" (Independent, 1996), and Etchingham's dossier led Scotland Yard to reopen the case. The record: Scotland Yard reopened its inquiries in December 1993. On March 8, 1994, the Attorney General rejected a new inquest, and the Independent reported in 1996 that the inquiry cleared Dannemann of delaying the ambulance. The open verdict stands.

Unproven claim: "Hendrix was murdered"

The claim: in his 2009 book Rock Roadie, former roadie James "Tappy" Wright wrote that manager Michael Jeffery confessed to him in 1971 that he had killed Hendrix, in order to collect on a life insurance policy (Guardian, June 2, 2009). The record: Jeffery died in a plane crash in 1973, so he could never answer the claim. The cause of death on record remains barbiturate intoxication and inhalation of vomit, under an open verdict. The claim rests on Wright's account and has never been proven.

Legend: the guitar burnings were spontaneous

The legend: that he set guitars alight in a moment of abandon. Melody Maker's front-page report at the time said the guitar "accidentally burst into flames" (quoted by Guitar World). The record: the first burning, at the Finsbury Park Astoria on March 31, 1967, was a planned press stunt. Journalist Keith Altham told NME (2017) that he suggested it in the dressing room and that Chandler sent for lighter fuel; Guitar World says he meant it as something of a joke. Accounts differ on the song: the official site says it happened during "Fire," and Guitar World quotes Altham's account in Q magazine naming "Wild Thing." At Monterey, Guitar World reports, the guitar was a prepared "sacrificial" Strat, set on fire in front of D.A. Pennebaker's film cameras. Chandler told NME (quoted by Guitar World) that the smashing routine itself "began by accident."

Legend: "the Daughters of the American Revolution got him thrown off the Monkees tour"

The legend: in July 1967, Melody Maker reported that the Daughters of the American Revolution had him dropped from the Monkees tour as "too erotic." The record: the official site says the story was "concocted" by a publicity team after Chandler and promoter Dick Clark agreed to pull him from the tour. He played eight shows. Hendrix told NME at the time (as quoted on the official site) that the audience was simply wrong for the band.
