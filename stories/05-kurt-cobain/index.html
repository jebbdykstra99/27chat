Kurt Cobain: Aberdeen to Nevermind and Unplugged

By Jebb Dykstra, Juno, Vesta and Iris

27chat.com · Story 05 · The work didn't stop.

This story follows Kurt Cobain's work with Nirvana, from Aberdeen to MTV Unplugged in New York, then sets the legends beside the record.

Where did Kurt Cobain start making music?

Cobain started in Aberdeen, Washington, where he was born on February 20, 1967 (Nirvana official timeline; Charles R. Cross, Seattle Times, 2001). Cross's biography says the Melvins drew him to punk and that he roadied for them. His first band, Fecal Matter, rehearsed in December 1985 with Dale Crover and Greg Hokanson and broke up without playing a gig (Cross). The official timeline says Cobain, Krist Novoselic and drummer Aaron Burckhard formed the first Nirvana lineup in Aberdeen in December 1987.

What was Bleach, Nirvana's first album?

Bleach was Nirvana's debut album, released by Sub Pop on June 15, 1989 (Sub Pop). It cost $606.17 to record, the New York Times reported, and guitarist Jason Everman paid for it, Novoselic told the paper (as quoted by Billboard, 2013). Cobain later told Rolling Stone (1994) that putting "About a Girl," a "jangly R.E.M. type of pop song," on a grunge record "was risky."

How did Nirvana and Butch Vig make Nevermind?

Butch Vig produced Nevermind at Sound City Studios in Van Nuys, California, and DGC released it on September 24, 1991 (Rolling Stone, 2013; official timeline). Vig told Rolling Stone that when "Something in the Way" kept coming out too aggressive, Cobain came into the control room and played it barely whispering, and "we built the whole song around it." It reached No. 1 on the Billboard 200 on January 11, 1992 (official timeline; Library of Congress).

Is Nevermind in the Library of Congress National Recording Registry?

Yes. Nevermind was added to the National Recording Registry in 2004 (Library of Congress). In the Registry essay, Josh Shepperd credits Vig's "lush, full, crystal production values" and Cobain's "jagged and memorable poetry."

How did Steve Albini record In Utero?

Steve Albini recorded and mixed In Utero at Pachyderm Recording Studio in Cannon Falls, Minnesota, between February 13 and 26, 1993 (Louder, 2023). It was released on September 21, 1993 (Louder; official timeline). Disputed: Albini says the released record "is precisely the record Nirvana wanted to make" (Louder, 2023). Cobain told Rolling Stone in 1994 that the vocals weren't loud enough, the bass was "totally inaudible," and some songs "could have been cleaned up."

What happened at MTV Unplugged in New York?

Nirvana taped MTV Unplugged at Sony Studios in New York in November 1993. The Guardian (2007) dates the taping to November 18; the official timeline says November 19. Six of the album's 14 tracks are covers, and Cris and Curt Kirkwood of the Meat Puppets joined for three Meat Puppets songs (Grammy.com; official timeline). MTV producer Alex Coletti told the Guardian that "everything was first take." The album came out in 1994, debuted at No. 1 and won the Grammy for Best Alternative Music Performance (Grammy.com).

How did Kurt Cobain write songs?

Cobain told Rolling Stone's David Fricke in 1994 that he usually started with the verse and then went into the chorus, a quiet-to-loud "formula" he said the band was tiring of. For "Smells Like Teen Spirit," he said, "I was trying to write the ultimate pop song. I was basically trying to rip off the Pixies," taking their "sense of dynamics." He called the riff "clichéd," close to Boston or "Louie, Louie."

What were Kurt Cobain's visual art and Journals?

Cobain won high-school art contests, passed on an art-school scholarship, and designed much of Nirvana's artwork, including the collage on the back cover of In Utero (Rolling Stone, 1994). His painting of a drooping, skeletal figure holding poppies became the cover of the 1992 compilation Incesticide (Seattle Times, 2017). Journals (Riverhead, November 2002) reproduces in facsimile pages from more than 20 spiral-bound notebooks: diary entries, lyrics, guitar chords, comic strips, letters and drafts of promotional material (Booklist, 2002).

When was Nirvana inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame?

Nirvana was inducted on April 10, 2014, at Barclays Center in Brooklyn, in its first year of eligibility (Rolling Stone, 2013; Barclays Center). Michael Stipe of R.E.M. inducted the band (Rock Hall).

How did Kurt Cobain die?

Kurt Cobain died by suicide at his home in Seattle in April 1994. He was 27. He was found on April 8; the King County Medical Examiner ruled the death a suicide, and police determined that he had died on April 5 (Seattle Times, 2014; UPI, 1994).

Kurt Cobain legends and disputed stories

Unproven claim: "Cobain was murdered"

The claim: Tom Grant, a former Los Angeles County Sheriff's detective hired in 1994 to find Cobain, has argued that the death was not a suicide. The 2015 documentary Soaked in Bleach presents his case, including the hypothesis that people close to Cobain arranged a killing (Variety, 2015). Nick Broomfield's 1998 film Kurt & Courtney, which Sundance pulled that January citing uncleared music rights, also featured Grant (Los Angeles Times). LA Times critic Kenneth Turan called the film "unreliable." The record: the medical examiner ruled the death a suicide. In 2014, Seattle Police cold-case detective Mike Ciesynski reviewed the file, including undeveloped film. He found no new information and did not reopen the case (Seattle Times, 2014). "It's a suicide. This is a closed case," he said (SPD Blotter). The murder claims have never been proven.

Legend: where "Smells Like Teen Spirit" got its name

The legend: that Tobi Vail wore Teen Spirit deodorant, as the Guardian reported in 2007, and that the phrase was spray-painted on Cobain's wall. The record: Kathleen Hanna told Double J (2016) that she and Vail had laughed at the deodorant in an Olympia grocery store, and that later that night she wrote "Kurt smells like Teen Spirit" on his wall in Sharpie. "He didn't know it was a deodorant," she said. She said the Vail story was "totally not true" and that "I never spray painted it."

Legend: he lived under a bridge in Aberdeen

The legend: that as a teenager without a home, Cobain lived under Aberdeen's Young Street Bridge, an image tied to "Something in the Way." Cross calls the story "one of his single most powerful pieces of myth-making." The record: "He never lived under that bridge," Novoselic told Cross. "He hung out there, but you couldn't live on those muddy banks." Cross writes that most nights he slept in the heated hallways of old apartment buildings.
